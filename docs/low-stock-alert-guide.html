Understanding Your
“Low Stock” Alerts

No formulas, no jargon. One idea explains every low-stock notification you will ever see — and it takes about twenty seconds to understand.

The one thing to remember

The number 0.5 means “half a week’s worth”

That is the whole rule.

01What the alert is actually saying

You sold a certain number of eggs in the last seven days. The 0.5 setting simply divides that number by two. Whatever you end up with is your warning line.

How many eggs you sold this week 1,551 ------------------------------------------------- Divide by 2 (because the setting says 0.5) 776 ← your warning line ------------------------------------------------- The rule: fewer than 776 eggs left → warn me

That is all a low-stock alert is. There is no hidden cleverness.

02Your real numbers

Using Medium (MD) from your own database:

Sold in last 7 days 1,551 eggs Warning line = 1,551 ÷ 2 = 776 eggs You actually have 6,900 eggs 6,900 is much more than 776 → nothing to worry about
Why you are not seeing alerts

You have roughly 31 days of stock on every egg size. The alert only wakes up when you are down to about 3 and a half days left. You are nowhere near that yet, so silence here is good news.

03Why “half a week” is the right moment to warn

You sell about 222 eggs a day. So the warning line is really a countdown:

776 eggs ÷ 222 a day = 3.5 days left

Read any alert as: “You only have a few days of eggs — time to restock.”

04Choosing your own number

Think of the setting as a dial for how much buffer you want to keep.

You setIt meansWarns you whenGood for
0.3one-third of a week2 days leftFast, reliable deliveries
0.5half a week3.5 days leftYour current setting
0.7most of a week5 days leftHard to find new stock

Lower number = warn later      Higher number = warn earlier

Restocking takes 2 days?  Use 0.3.
Restocking takes 3–4 days?  Use 0.5.
Restocking takes over 5 days?  Use 0.7.

05What you will see when it fires

1
The warning line is worked out
Your week’s sales are halved. That number is the line.
2
Your stock is measured against it
Less than the line means the alert appears.
3
You are told how many days are left
Not eggs — days. That is the number to think in.
4
You are told how many trays to buy
So the alert is already a shopping list, not just a worry.

06An example, start to finish

Suppose your Medium stock drops to 690 eggs and sales have not changed:

Step 1 Warning line 1,551 ÷ 2 = 776 eggs Step 2 Compare 690 is less than 776 = LOW STOCK Step 3 Days left 690 ÷ 222 a day = 3.1 days Step 4 What is missing 776 − 690 = 86 eggs short Step 5 What to buy 86 ÷ 30 per tray = 2.9 → buy 3 trays
That is the entire alert

Low stock: Medium — 690 eggs left (about 3.1 days of cover).
Check: 690 < 776 cutoff = 1,551 sold in 7d × 0.5 threshold.
Short by 86 eggs — buy 3 trays to clear it.

07Two special cases

SituationWhat happens
You have zero eggs HIGH ALERT  Always fires, whatever the setting is. There is nothing to sell at all, so it skips the calculation entirely.
A quiet week If a size sold nothing in the last 7 days, the system uses the forecast instead. The alert tells you which one it used.

08How to change it

  1. Open the Settings page.
  2. Find the row called low_stock_threshold.
  3. Type a decimal — 0.6, not 60.
  4. Press Update. It takes effect straight away.
Two helpful things to look for

1. The Settings page now shows a “What low stock actually means here” panel listing every egg size with its stock, its weekly sales, its warning line and how many days of cover that is.

2. Allowed values run from 0.1 to 1.0. Anything outside that range is quietly pulled back to the nearest limit.

If you remember nothing else

Divide this week’s sales by two.
Fewer eggs than that?  Time to restock.
